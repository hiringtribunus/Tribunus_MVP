Our Wedge: Approval Certainty for BC Developers

A plan to focus the MVP on the one job we can truly own, with the reasoning and the market data behind it.

August 2026 · Internal working plan
In one line

Tribunus becomes the always current, cited source of truth on what will get approved and what it will cost, for large developers in British Columbia.

The principle

Why we commit to one wedge

An early product wins by being a must have for one buyer on one job. It does not win by being broad. Our current feature list quietly served three different jobs at once, for slightly different buyers, against three different sets of competitors. To become indispensable, we commit to one job now and treat the other two as the path we grow into later.

The choice

Three jobs were hiding in our product

Each one answers a different question a developer asks at a different moment. We pick the middle one.

Entry point
Should we buy this land?

Fast screening before a purchase: zoning, fees, and a quick read on whether the numbers work. The field is crowded with tools developers already trust. We keep only its strongest parts as a way in, not as the product.

The wedge
Will this get approved, and what will it really cost?

Reducing the risk on the path from concept to municipal approval, with every fact cited to a source. This is open field. No one serves BC developers here today. This is where we win.

Later
Keep the live file on track.

Tracking consultants, city comments, and deadlines once a project is underway. This is project management software, a crowded field with a different shape. It becomes expansion once the wedge is proven.

The evidence

Why this wedge, in numbers

13 to 14 months
Average time to approve a rezoning or development permit in BC. (CHBA)
Longest in Canada
Vancouver has the longest approval delays in the country. (CMHC)
Up to 10x
How much city fees on a new home can vary between municipalities.
15 to 30 percent
Share of a project budget that is soft costs, spent mostly before construction.
Zero
Direct competitors serving BC developers with cited approval intelligence that we could find.
$2.2 billion
Proptech funding in 2024. Zoning and permitting is a hot, funded category.

The rules just changed, and keep changing

Bills 44, 46, and 47 rewrote the rules in 2023. The rollout was so uneven the province passed Bill 25 in 2025 to close loopholes, and cities are now challenging it in court. No person can hold the current rules across every city in their head.

The pain is expensive

Delay is carrying cost on the land loan. In one reported case a project rose by roughly $300,000 during a single 13 month wait. Uncertainty on fees and pathway can flip a deal.

The budget already exists

Developers pay planners, engineers, and expediters today to reduce exactly this risk. Soft costs already run to millions on a large project. The money is in the system.

The open space is local

The funded players are all based in the US, and their zoning is not our zoning. Depth in British Columbia is the moat, and the reason no one can copy us overnight.

The plan

How we refine the product for this wedge

We start from the consolidated list of 19 features and sort every one into three buckets: build it for the pilot, add it because the wedge needs it, or defer it because it belongs to another job.

Keep · the pilot
  • Verified Site Baseline
  • Fees and Contributions Review
  • Run Development Review (risks, pathway, timeline, readiness)
  • Findings Register (assumptions and risks)
  • Precedent and Council Intelligence
  • Project Watch and Alerts (portfolio monitoring matters for this buyer)
  • Reports and Export
Plus the trust and foundation layer that makes these real: accounts, create project, documents, profile, calculators, municipal data, citations, analyst review, and the portfolio home.
Add · sharpen the wedge
  • Feedback and Outcome Capture. Record whether a finding was right, how the city actually responded, and the final result. This proves we are a must have and builds a data set no competitor has.
  • A thin economics bridge. Not a full modeling tool. A simple link that turns a fee or a risk into its effect on cost per buildable square foot or margin, so a finding connects to the number a developer decides on.
Defer · another job
  • Consultant Requirement Checklist (project management, wedge C)
  • City Comment Management (wedge C). Keep only the light comment extraction if it feeds the review.
  • Local Sales Comps and Feasibility (wedge A). Replaced in the pilot by the thin economics bridge.
  • Trim the placeholders to a few that show the roadmap: Ask This Project, Scenario Comparison, and the Analysis Engine.
Our recommendation

The three open decisions, recommended

These are our proposed answers, offered for a decision together, not a done deal.

How far into the numbers?
A thin bridge. Connect findings to cost and margin, but leave full modeling to the tools developers already use.
What do we cut to stay focused?
The project management features. They are a different job and a crowded field, and they do not compound our intelligence advantage.
What do we add?
The feedback and outcome loop. It is how we prove the product changes real decisions, and how we build a data moat.

The bet

Narrow and indispensable beats broad and impressive. We aim to become the one tool a large BC developer will not underwrite a deal without, in a rare window while the rules are still in motion. Then we grow, because one more city is a data slice, not a rebuild.
